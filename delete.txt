We conduct a comprehensive empirical evaluation of PPO+ against related on-policy baselines PPO and TRPO, and include SAC as a strong baseline. For all algorithms using maximum entropy, we use automatic temperature tuning similar to \citep{haarnoja2018soft}. The evaluation is performed on the MuJoCo benchmark suite \citep{todorov2012mujoco} and high-dimensional DOG tasks from DM-Control \citep{tunyasuvunakool2020}. Our baseline implementations are based on \citep{huang2022cleanrl}. To ensure fairness, we introduce PPO(ours) as a variant using similar configuration to \algname and tune all baselines \textbf{MENTION APPENDIX}. Consistent with our ablation study, we use two critic networks trained independently without minimum target approach. Key implementation differences are summarized in Table \ref{table:difference_implementation} and hyperparameter settings in Table \ref{tab:hyper} in Appendix \ref{A:hyper}.

Figure \ref{fig:benchmark_1} shows that \algname~attains performance comparable to PPO on simple tasks like Hopper and Walker, while significantly outperforming PPO on complex, high-dimensional tasks like HalfCheetah, Ant and Humanoid. We attribute this gap to PPO's advantage estimation limitations, which requires high lambda values ($\lambda=0.95$) making its value estimator close to a Monte Carlo estimate that is less effective for high-dimensional environments.

Although \algname~lags behind SAC on MuJoCo tasks, particularly for Humanoid, Figure \ref{fig:benchmark_2} reveals this dynamic reverses on higher-dimensional DOG tasks, where our algorithm performs significantly better.

We hypothesize this difference may be attributed to the nature of exploration each algorithm promotes, supported by our ablation studies (Figure \ref{fig:ablation}). The Humanoid task benefits from more aggressive exploration, explaining SAC's effectiveness as its off-policy updates' variance induces noisy exploration. For higher-dimensional DOG tasks, this noise becomes a hindrance to stable learning, where \algname~high quality gradient from its well-trained critic and on-policy data proves more advantageous.

In summary, PPO+ consistently outperforms on-policy algorithms like PPO and TRPO, and significantly closes the performance gap to leading off-policy methods such as SAC, especially in complex, high-dimensional control tasks. \algname~achieves this advantage over PPO without implementation-level optimizations \ref{tab:comparison}, showcasing our approach's robustness.
